Our Story

Neither of us has lived a conventional life.

Like many of the people we work with, we've experienced major transitions, unexpected challenges, and moments when the path ahead wasn't clear. Between us, we've navigated divorce, blended family dynamics, neurodiversity, career changes, financial obstacles, long-distance love, and the ongoing process of redefining what a meaningful life looks like.

Those experiences taught us something important:

The greatest transformations don't come from trying to become someone else. They come from understanding who you truly are and building a life that reflects it.

Long before we met, we were each drawn to that work in our own way.

Nicole spent years exploring personal growth, fitness, human behavior, relationships, spirituality, and the question at the heart of so many people's struggles: "What do I actually want?" Her work helps people uncover the desires, values, strengths, and dreams that often get buried beneath expectations, obligations, and old conditioning.

Casey's path led him toward systems, problem-solving, communication, neurodiversity, financial coaching, and helping people navigate life's practical challenges with greater clarity and confidence. He brings a grounded perspective that turns insight into action.

They met in a Facebook group called Divorced, Single, and Loving It. The last place either of them expected to meet the one.

Nicole caught Casey's eye early. He never reached out privately, because he did not want to be a creep about it.

Then Nicole and a few others in the group organized a virtual wellness event for single parents. Casey signed up, not knowing she was one of the organizers. She messaged him to send the Zoom information, and that was their first private conversation.

He kept talking to her after that, and it turned out they had the same thing at the center of their lives: traveling with their kids and personal growth. She helped him plan his first road trip with the boys and gave him every single parent travel tip she had.

He sent her pictures afterward and told her what a great time they'd had. She could see he was serious about it, so she offered her apartment if he ever wanted to bring them to New York.

There were no intentions of romance in it. Just a friendly offer. But they hit it off.

Even then, a long distance relationship seemed like a bad idea, and neither of them intended for it to become one. They kept coming back to each other anyway, like magnets, and 8 months after meeting in person they were in a relationship whether it made sense or not.

The kids got there first. At different points, separately, they told Casey and Nicole they should marry each other. This was before any of them knew their parents were dating.

After several more family trips and a few romantic getaways, Casey proposed at Christmas Eve dinner in 2025.

What we learned along the way is that our approaches complement each other.

One of us naturally starts with meaning, purpose, and self-discovery. The other naturally starts with structure, strategy, and implementation.

Together, we help bridge the gap between inner transformation and real-world change.

Because insight alone isn't enough. And action without self-understanding often leads people toward goals they don't truly want.

Whether we're supporting someone through a relationship challenge, a major life transition, personal growth, neurodiversity, financial decisions, or simply the feeling that life could be more aligned, our philosophy remains the same:

You don't need to become someone new. You need to better understand who you are, what matters most to you, and how to build a life that reflects it.

We're still on that journey ourselves. And that's part of what makes this work meaningful. Not because we have all the answers, but because we've spent years asking the questions that matter.

We Believe

  • There is no single right way to live.
  • Creating your dream life starts with becoming an aligned version of yourself.
  • Great relationships are built, not discovered.
  • Neurodiversity is a difference to understand, not a flaw to fix.
  • Small consistent actions create lasting change.
  • Physical health, mental health, relationships. It's all connected.

How we got here

December 2023, Colonial Williamsburg

The last stop on that drive. Christmas week in a town that has not changed in three hundred years, with four kids who could not stand still for one minute of it.

December 2024, Washington DC

The next Christmas we did it again, by train. Nicole came into this with a tradition where the elf and Santa publish a book about the trip they planned, and the kid finds it wrapped under the tree. We have our own elf now. This year the book was a spiral-bound Amtrak guide to Washington.

None of this was smooth. We built it across two states, two households, four kids, and two people who had both been through a divorce and had every reason to be careful. This is what it has actually looked like: not a clean break from the past, but a long run of ordinary days where we chose differently than we were taught to, in front of children who were watching.
