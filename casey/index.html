Casey Connell

Coach & Mediator

Debt and divorce are two of the most stressful experiences a person can go through, and they often arrive at the same time.

Debt at its worst is relentless. Payments go out every month and the balance barely moves. Interest compounds faster than income grows. Bills get timed to paydays. A single unexpected expense, a repair, a medical bill, a cut in hours, can take the whole structure down. The stress is constant and it is expensive, because the longer it goes unresolved the more it costs.

Casey Connell's work starts there. He builds an accurate picture of what is owed, at what rate, and what it is actually costing. Then he decides with the client which debts to shed, which to restructure, which to pay down, and in what order. The first goal is making the next ninety days survivable. The second is making the next year workable. He knows options for reducing bad debt that most coaches never encounter, and the difference between shedding a debt well and badly can be years of recovery time.

Not everyone comes in under pressure. Some have income and ambition and a credit profile that cannot support what they want to do next. That shows up as denials, as rates several points higher than advertised, as larger deposits and shorter terms, and as tens of thousands of dollars in additional cost over the life of a loan. It is a solvable problem with a clear sequence. Casey builds the scores and the cash flow that change the terms available, until the client is setting them rather than accepting them.

Optimization is not a phase that begins after the emergency ends. It is the work at every stage. Only the target moves.

Divorce brings the legal, the financial, and the emotional together at once. Attorneys bill by the hour whether the hour is spent on strategy or on venting. Decisions get made under time pressure with consequences that last twenty years: who keeps the house, how retirement is divided, what the parenting schedule looks like in practice. Communication with the other person gets worse exactly when it needs to get better. Sleep suffers, and the decisions do not wait for it.

Casey builds the real financial picture first, including what two households actually cost, which is the number most people never calculate before negotiating. He prepares clients for attorney meetings so the billable hour goes to strategy. He helps write the messages that lower the temperature instead of raising it, and builds parenting plans that fit a real life rather than a template. As a mediator he sits between two people and stays neutral. As a coach he is in one person's corner. He does not do both in the same divorce.

He is a certified divorce mediator and a personal finance coach, with a background in engineering and science at a national laboratory that shows up in the work: methodically, numbers on the table, nothing hidden. He is the father of three sons, part of a family that includes members with special needs, and neurodivergent himself. That shapes what he builds. Most financial and co-parenting plans assume a level of consistency that is difficult to sustain through a hard month, so his are designed to hold up during one.

In 2025 he published a memoir, Why You Keep Choosing the Same Pain, about the patterns people inherit and what it takes to break them. He grew up in a house he would not wish on anyone and spent years learning how not to hand it down. His tattoo reads "It ends with me."

The goal is not to help people survive a hard season. It is to get them out the other side clearer, steadier, holding better terms than they walked in with, and no longer repeating the thing that brought them here.
