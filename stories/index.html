If you want generational trauma to end with you, it takes more than working on your thoughts and behavior.

Kids feel safety and unsafety in us. No matter how well we fake it.

If we're chronically dysregulated, they feel that

And pairing dysfunction with a fake smile and calling it happiness? That doesn’t fool them.

Their developing mind learns: “This is what it means to feel okay."

It's not our fault. We were set up for failure. I've been the fake-happy zombie mom more times than I can count.

But better is possible. I've worked for years to achieve it.

It's the ability to regulate ourselves, then use that calm, centered state to co-regulate with our kids, bringing them back to safety in hard moments.

It's the ability to experience trauma without becoming traumatized.

To bounce back to a peaceful baseline quickly, and lead our kids to do the same.

This is what we practice in Catharsis, a monthly class in self-regulation and co-regulation.

I share the tools. We experiment with them together.

Beginners start gently. Experienced practitioners push further, intentionally activating intense sensation to build their capacity.

Either way, we practice navigating our own experience first, then have the option to pair up for partner practices.

We learn to consciously feel and read another person's state, and notice their impact on us without being sucked into their negative emotions.

We cultivate a presence solid enough to hold someone else's distress.

These are the same co-regulation skills I use with my son, my partner, my loved ones, even strangers, to help them find calm and clarity in hard moments.

If you want generational trauma to end with you, come practice releasing it from your nervous system.

And learn how to bring your kids back to peace, faster.

Nicole · August 2026

My partner charged toward them, seeing red and jaw twitching, with two muscular fitness trainers trying unsuccessfully to hold him back.

It was shortly after Hurricane Sandy and my husband at the time and I had just lost our home.

In Rockaway, NYC we don’t need a car, but staying with my parents in Long Island we had bought an old one in cash to get back and forth to work.

A couple had tried to turn their car around and cut off a bus. The bus hit them and propelled their car into our parked car.

It was too much during an already devastating time and he lost it. His higher self blacked out and the animal mind took over.

I ran out in front of him and put my hand on his arm.

“Honey, they just got hit by a bus,” I said calmly but with power.

I quickly turned to the couple. “Is the baby ok?”

My partner suddenly snapped out of it like he was coming out of a daze, the Hulk returning to Dr. Banner.

“Uh…yeah, is the baby ok?”

I didn’t know what it was called at the time, but that’s the power of emotional co-regulation.

It involves using your own calm, centered state to help others - often those in distress - return to a calmer, more centered state.

It wasn’t an accident, and it’s not something I was born with.

It’s a skill.

I use it with Casey, Eric, other kids, friends, clients, and strangers.

As I heard the engine rev up for takeoff a memory bubbled up.

It was Eric’s first flight. He was 3. When he heard the engine rev up he got excited.

“It’s the loud noise! We’re gonna take off now!”

It’s mind blowing how impactful it is for little kids when we give them enough information to understand what’s happening.

Think about it.

Little kids spend much of the time having no clue what’s happening or why.

Think about how excited they get for some routines, like TV shows that have them say the same thing each time. They know what to expect. They got this!

This trick helped me tremendously when he was little and I was traveling as a single mom with a low budget.

In this case, to prepare for his first flight I found a kid-friendly video in which a woman explained everything that happens on a flight, from navigating the airport to finding your seat to what sounds to expect.

He watched that video over and over again.

Then, when we traveled he was able to understand what was happening and create meaning from what he observed.

When the engine got louder, he knew what was going to happen next.

And he got excited.

This worked even when the event was less exciting.

I remember our first Christmas Train Adventure.

Santa left a book (“published” by Staples 😉) under the tree with all the details.

You should have seen how excited he got when he saw the sign for Fredericksburg, Virginia, and when we arrived at a dirty 2-star motel.

It was what I could afford and at 5 years old he didn’t know that it wasn’t great.

But having the book with a page about Fredericksburg and a picture of the motel made it the most exciting thing ever.

Later along our adventure, you should have heard our previously silent train car giggle when a tiny voice screamed, “It’s the Washington Monument!” as we approached DC.

This trick isn’t only great for the kid.

Happy, engaged kids tend to be better behaved which makes travel smoother for everybody.

Now Eric is a pro and the best travel buddy ever. On some trips he even does most of the planning.

The dating space is pretty clearly a dumpster fire across the board.

Men are angry at women and women are angry with men.

The resentment from having to do that for so long & from men having little to no guidance to grow our emotional intelligence leaves a lot of frustration in the dating and relationship space.

Men need more guides and a willingness to adjust to the new culture in dating and courtship and a drastically different approach.

Most women are looking for emotional intelligence which isn't natural to most of us, myself included. Many women are frustrated by years if not decades of most of us not learning this skill.

Women, well, we can start with the space to do the necessary work. By that I mean not berating men who aren't aware the work is necessary. This is a honey approach over vinegar. It's not easy, but generally speaking, the vinegar approach has helped lead us to this dumpster fire.

Women, what also comes with that emotional intelligence is a requirement that you have the capacity for hearing some really gnarly stuff that goes through men’s minds. Many of you aren't ready for the unfiltered access to men’s emotional states many of you are asking for.

Rather than trashing them for it, we should be clear that those don't align with the kind of partner we want and wish them well finding someone that works for.

When you find the right one, with mutual consent, don't do silly things to ruin it.
